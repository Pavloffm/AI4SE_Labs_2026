```prompt
You are a [ROLE] tasked with solving the following problem: [PROBLEM].

1. Generate three genuinely different solutions to the problem.
2. Briefly explain how each solution would work.
3. Evaluate each solution based on cost, time required, feasibility, potential benefits, and risks. Score each criterion from 1 to 5 and justify the scores.
4. Eliminate the weakest solution and explain what would need to change for it to become viable.
5. Compare the two remaining solutions in greater detail.
6. Recommend the best solution and explain why it is preferable to the alternatives.
7. Identify any assumptions and explain what new information could change your recommendation.

Output format: Use clear headings, a comparison table, and a final recommendation.
```

- This one, interested me, because it can be used in telling someone about my projects and other things and can be helpful in it. Also it can help me to understand problem :>
```prompt
You are a problem-solving consultant. Analyze the following problem: [PROBLEM].

Structure your response using the STAR framework:

1. Situation: Describe the context of the problem and explain why it matters.
2. Task: Identify the main objective and the specific responsibilities involved.
3. Action: Propose concrete actions that could be taken to address the problem. Explain the steps in a logical order.
4. Result: Describe the expected outcomes and suggest measurable indicators that could be used to evaluate success.

Clearly distinguish between facts, assumptions, and expected outcomes. Do not invent data or present hypothetical results as actual achievements.

Output format: Four clearly labeled sections, followed by a short conclusion explaining how the proposed actions could solve the problem.
```

- Find how to get to the goal and evaluate is it really work in correct way of metrics. Find how many time it can spent.
```prompt
You are an experienced project manager. Help me establish clear goals for the following project: [PROJECT DESCRIPTION].

Use the SMART framework to develop an effective project goal:

1. Specific: Define exactly what the project aims to achieve.
2. Measurable: Identify measurable indicators that will show whether the goal has been achieved.
3. Achievable: Assess whether the goal is realistic given the available resources, skills, and constraints.
4. Relevant: Explain how the goal supports the broader objectives of the project or organization.
5. Time-bound: Set a realistic deadline and identify important milestones.

If essential information is missing, state your assumptions and identify what needs clarification. Do not invent facts about available resources or project constraints.

Finish by providing one concise SMART goal statement and a checklist for evaluating progress.

Output format: Five SMART criteria, a final goal statement, and a progress checklist.
```
